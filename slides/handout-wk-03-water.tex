{\sffamily\Large\bfseries Reading each other's writing}

\vspace{0.5ex}
{\sffamily\large Water use in data centres}

\vspace{1ex}
{\footnotesize Writer: \rule{45mm}{0.4pt}\qquad Reader:
  \rule{45mm}{0.4pt}\qquad Open Knowledge, \dateSources}

\vspace{2ex}
Read the whole thing once before you write anything.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.6ex,topsep=1.6ex]

\item \textbf{What is this writer's claim?}
  {\footnotesize One sentence, in your own words --- not a summary of the three
    texts.}
  \lines{3}

\item \textbf{Which of the three texts did they trust most, and on what
    grounds?}
  {\footnotesize Name the grounds: evidence, sources, hedging, tone.}
  \lines{3}

\item \textbf{Do you agree with their judgement of the sources?}
  {\footnotesize Point at a sentence.}
  \lines{4}

\item \textbf{Do you agree with their conclusion about water?}
  \lines{4}

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{1ex}
\hrule
\vspace{1ex}

{\footnotesize \textbf{3 and 4 are different questions.}  A text can be well
  evidenced and still reach a conclusion you reject, and you can agree with a
  conclusion that rests on nothing.  Say which is which.\\[0.6ex]
  \textbf{Then talk to the writer.}  Start with question 1, and let them tell
  you whether that is what they meant.}
