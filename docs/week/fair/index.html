FAIR: making data findable and reusable

27 October, 16:45-18:15.

This week is the first half of sharing research responsibly: FAIR — Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable. The slides give more detail, as well as references. The ethics of sharing — who it helps and who it can harm — is next week.

Key topics

  • FAIR principles — why each one matters:
    • Findable: so others (and your future self) can actually discover the work.
    • Accessible: so once found, the work can be retrieved safely and clearly.
    • Interoperable: so work can combine with other work across tools and languages.
    • Reusable: so the community can build on it—thanks to documentation and licensing.
  • Open licences and the Open Definition; when and when not to use open licences.
  • Routes to open access — gold, green, diamond, and what each costs whom.

In class

Bring your annotated bibliography. We audit one source each for FAIR problems — can you find it by DOI or stable URL, can you read it, is its licence clear? — and decide whether to keep it, replace it, or cite it with its limitations stated.

Why this matters

FAIR practice makes research verifiable and sustainable: work that others can find, check and build on.
